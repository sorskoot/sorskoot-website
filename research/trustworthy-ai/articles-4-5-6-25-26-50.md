Each page says it is "based on the EUR-Lex consolidated version of the AI Act as at 27 July 2026" and marks Omnibus changes as NEW or AMENDED. Binding text says "shall"; the non-binding page summaries sometimes say "should".

## Article 4 (AI literacy, AMENDED)
- Para 1: providers and deployers "shall take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of staff and others operating AI on their behalf, "taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training and the context".
- "This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any individual."
- Para 2: "the Commission shall publish practical examples of how to comply".
- Para 3: "The Board shall adopt recommendations, taking into account European competence frameworks."

## Article 5 (prohibited practices)
- (a) subliminal or manipulative techniques; (b) exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, social or economic situation); (c) social scoring; (d) criminal-offence risk assessment based solely on profiling or personality traits; (e) untargeted scraping for facial recognition databases; (f) emotion inference in workplaces and education, except medical or safety reasons; (g) biometric categorisation inferring sensitive traits; (h) real-time remote biometric identification for law enforcement in public spaces, with listed exceptions.
- NEW (ba): realistic intimate or sexually explicit images or videos of an identifiable person without explicit consent. NEW (bb): child sexual abuse material. Page summary: "From 2 December 2026, two additional prohibitions apply."
- The fine level (EUR 35M or 7%) is not on this page (it is in Art. 99, not fetched). It is stated in ai-practices-guidelines.md.

## Article 6 (classification)
- 6(3): an Annex III system "shall not be considered to be high-risk where it does not pose a significant risk of harm to the health, safety or fundamental rights of natural persons, including by not materially influencing the outcome of decision making." Conditions (a) narrow procedural task; (b) improves the result of a previously completed human activity; (c) detects patterns or deviations and "is not meant to replace or influence the previously completed human assessment, without proper human review"; (d) preparatory task.
- "an AI system referred to in Annex III shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs profiling of natural persons."
- 6(4): a provider that judges an Annex III system not high-risk must document the assessment and register it (Art. 49(2)).

## Article 25 (value chain)
- 25(1): a deployer or other third party becomes the provider of a high-risk system if (a) they put their name or trademark on it, (b) "they make a substantial modification", or (c) "they modify the intended purpose of an AI system, including a general-purpose AI system, which has not been classified as high-risk" so that it becomes high-risk.
- 25(2) (AMENDED): the original provider "shall no longer be considered to be a provider" but must cooperate and provide documentation and technical access.

## Article 26 (deployers of high-risk systems)
- 26(1) use "in accordance with the instructions for use".
- 26(2) assign human oversight to people with "the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support."
- 26(5) monitor operation, inform the provider, suspend use on risk; report serious incidents.
- 26(6) keep automatically generated logs "of at least six months".
- 26(7) before workplace use, employers "shall inform workers' representatives and the affected workers".
- Also: 26(9) use information for a DPIA; 26(11) inform people subject to Annex III decisions.

## Article 50 (transparency)
- 50(1): providers must design systems so people "are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious" to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person.
- 50(2): providers of synthetic audio, image, video or text must ensure outputs are "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated." Exemption for assistive standard-editing functions or systems that do not "substantially alter the input data".
- 50(3): deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation inform exposed people.
- 50(4) deepfakes: deployers "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated"; for "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work" disclosure must not hamper display or enjoyment.
- 50(4) text: AI text "published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest shall disclose" it, except where it "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility".
- 50(5): information "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure."
- The page states no application date for Art. 50.
